Reference architecture of the crypto-agile migration framework as implemented
by the simulator, drawn as four tiers inside the implementation boundary and
the encrypted carrier services that they protect below it. Top tier: the
service inventory supplies the per-service context, i.e.\ secrecy lifetime,
migration lead time and planning horizon, from which the Mosca margin
(lifetime plus lead time minus horizon) and the HNDL exposure (a positive
margin) are derived, as well as SLA class, capability set and fail-closed
flag, so that every decision is made per service rather than per physical
link. Second tier (tinted): the crypto-agile policy engine prioritises
services by their Mosca margin (a service is HNDL-exposed when the margin is
positive), selects an admissible mode along the ladder Traditional
$\rightarrow$ PQC $\rightarrow$ PQ/T hybrid $\rightarrow$ QKD-assisted
$\rightarrow$ PQ/T\,+\,QKD, forecasts the QKD reserve time and applies
re-entry hysteresis ($\kappa = 1.5$), and enforces the fail-closed downgrade
rules (an HNDL-exposed fail-closed service never falls back to Traditional
only); its selective overlay planner qualifies candidate QKD routes against
the designed hop limit, the trusted-node policy limit and the planning floor
$R_{\min}$ (reference values 80~km, 4 trusted nodes and 100~bit/s) and
selects the QKD overlay links. Third tier: the key sources are PQ/T hybrid
key establishment (ML-KEM\,+\,ECDH) and the ETSI-014-inspired paired-KME
pool (key identifiers, matching peer retrieval, single-use semantics,
explicit errors), which the QKD links (direct spans or trusted-node routes)
refill at the planning-envelope secret-key rate; the reserve-time forecast
treats the protected reserve, a fraction $\beta = 0.05$ of the pool capacity
$B_{\max}$, as unusable, and the paired-KME behaviour is isolated from the
protocol-specific adapters of the fourth tier. Fourth tier: the protocol
adapters and binding profiles, i.e.\ IKEv2 with RFC~9370 additional key
exchanges and RFC~9867 PPK mixing, and generic MACsec/MKA and OTN-encryptor
abstractions that are not claims of standardised PQC/QKD bindings; each
adapter rekeys the carrier service drawn below it (IPsec tunnels, Ethernet
secure associations, optical channels). Solid arrows carry planning and
control information and key material (service context; QKD overlay links;
mode decision and key requests; refill; keys and key IDs; rekey), the arrows
inside the policy engine give the order of the decision chain, and the
dashed path on the right returns telemetry (pool level, predicted reserve
time, outages, rekey outcomes) from the adapters and, joining at the dot,
from the key sources to the reserve-time forecast. The grey dashed rounded
rectangle marks the scope of the reference implementation: everything inside
it is exercised by the seeded synthetic scenario (topology seed 7, simulation
seed 11; 35 links, 60 services), the carrier services below it are
represented only by the analytical rekey outcomes of their adapters, and no
carrier data are used anywhere in the evaluation.
